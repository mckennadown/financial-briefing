Developments
Canada

TSX has its worst week since March 2020

What happened: The TSX fell 838.88 points on Friday to 33,189.85, The Canadian Press reported. That's a drop of nearly 2.5% and its seventh loss in a row. The index is down 8.8% since Nov. 11. The Motley Fool Canada said it lost 8.1% over the week, its worst week since March 2020. Mining, tech and financial stocks fell the most. Gold fell US$170.10 to US$3,515.40 an ounce.
Why it matters: Mining companies make up a big part of the TSX, so falling metal prices hit Canadian stocks hard. Fund manager John Zechner told CP the market was "crowded." In other words, too many investors owned the same tech stocks. When those stocks fell, many investors sold at once.
What it means for you: If you own a Canadian index fund, it has dropped quickly. Sharp drops like this are a normal part of investing, even if they're painful. Selling in a panic locks in losses. If you need the money soon, though, it's worth checking that it isn't all in stocks.
US / Global

The S&P 500 falls 3.7% in one day, and the Nasdaq is down for 2026

What happened: The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday to 6,910.39, AP reported. That left it down 8.6% for the week. The Nasdaq fell 5.3%, and it's now down 2.7% for 2026. Anthropic's shares fell 9.1% to US$88.30 a day after its record-setting IPO (a company's first sale of shares to the public), CP reported. That's still above the US$83 IPO price.
Why it matters: A drop of 10% or more from a recent high is called a "correction." The Nasdaq fell about 10% in a single week. CP reported that investors now expect US interest rate hikes instead of cuts. Higher US rates tend to strengthen the US dollar and weaken the loonie.
What it means for you: Many Canadians own US tech stocks through ETFs, mutual funds and pensions. Those holdings have fallen fast. A weaker loonie also makes US trips and US-priced goods more expensive.
Canada

Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem will step down when his term ends

What happened: Macklem said on Nov. 16 that he will leave when his term ends, The Canadian Press reported. The next governor's seven-year term starts June 3, 2027. The bank's board has started its search and expects to finish by spring 2027. The finance minister and federal cabinet must approve the choice. CP named Senior Deputy Governor Carolyn Rogers as a possible successor.
Why it matters: The governor leads the decisions on interest rates. Those decisions affect mortgage rates, loan costs and savings rates for every Canadian. Macklem speaks to the Economic Club of Canada on Tuesday, Nov. 24, about the economy and the challenges of setting rates.
What it means for you: Nothing changes right away. Macklem still leads the next rate decisions, starting Dec. 9. If you're renewing a mortgage soon, Tuesday's speech may hint at where rates are heading.
VC / Private Markets

Ottawa defence startup Dominion Dynamics teams up with big partners on a fighter drone

What happened: Dominion Dynamics has committed $50 million to build "Dominion Scout," BetaKit reported. It's a small drone without a pilot, designed to fly alongside Canadian fighter jets. A dozen companies, including Pratt & Whitney Canada and Telesat, have agreed to help. The company also has a $17-million federal contract to build a drone that finds sea mines for the navy. It has raised $169 million since it launched in June 2025.
Why it matters: BetaKit said the navy contract may be the first big government commitment to a Canadian defence startup. Canada plans to spend more on defence. Startups like this could keep more of that money, and the jobs it creates, in Canada.
What it means for you: Defence is quickly becoming one of the hottest areas for Canadian venture capital (money invested in young private companies). Expect more funding news, and more tech jobs, in this area.

Learn One Thing Today
Correction vs. Bear Market
A "correction" is when a stock index falls at least 10% from its recent high. A "bear market" is a bigger drop of 20% or more. Last week, the Nasdaq fell about 10% in five days. Here's a simple example: if a fund hit $100 a unit and then dropped to $89, it's in a correction. If it dropped to $79, it's in a bear market.
