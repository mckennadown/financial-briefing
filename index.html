Developments
Canada

Canada's central bank raised interest rates for the first time in 3 years

What happened: On Oct 28, the Bank of Canada bumped its key rate up a notch, to 2.50%, because rising oil prices are pushing inflation back up. Most experts think they'll pause at their next meeting on Dec 9.
Why it matters: For years rates were flat or falling — this is the first sign the Bank is worried about prices rising again.
What it means for you: Borrowing (mortgages, credit lines) gets a little pricier. Savings accounts and GICs should pay a bit more interest.
VC / Private Markets

More money is going into Canadian startups than it has in years

What happened: Canadian startups raised $2.69 billion in the first half of 2026 — 17% more than last year, and the first increase in 5 years.
Why it matters: After a long slump, investors are warming back up to Canadian startups.
What it means for you: Good news if you're connected to startups — more funding floating around usually means more jobs and opportunities in that world.
Canada

One of Canada's biggest VC firms just raised $200 million for AI bets

What happened: Inovia Capital closed the first $200M chunk of a new fund focused on AI startups.
Why it matters: Big Canadian investors are still able to raise money, even though fundraising has been tough globally.
What it means for you: More cash chasing Canadian AI companies — could push their valuations up.
Global / Markets

Stocks bounced back hard after a rough week

What happened: The TSX and major US markets jumped on Nov 9, recovering from a week where tech stocks (Nasdaq) had dropped over 3%.
Why it matters: Big swings up and down lately mean markets are nervous right now, not calm.
What it means for you: If you have investments, don't be surprised by more ups and downs than usual — try not to overreact to any single day.
What to Watch Next
Dec 9 — Bank of Canada's next rate decision: will they pause, as most economists expect, or hike again? That will set the tone for borrowing costs into 2027.
Continued market swings: after last week's drop and this week's rally, expect choppier-than-normal trading to keep going in the near term.
Bond yield data gap: the 10-year Government of Canada yield wasn't available today — worth checking tomorrow, since it moves alongside mortgage rates.
Learn One Thing Today
Basis Point (bps)
A tiny unit used for interest rates: 1 basis point = 0.01%. So when the Bank of Canada raises rates by "25 basis points," that just means +0.25%. Bankers use this instead of percentages because it avoids confusion — "raised by 25%" would sound like a huge jump, but "25bps" is precise and small.
